# Fourteen insurers supply Japan's single-premium shelf

As of 16 November 2026, **14 of the 41 life insurers** on the Life Insurance Association member list have an in-scope single-premium savings product on sale through a third-party channel, and two more (Taiyo, Taiju) have only borderline single-premium endowments. The reader's working list of ten is **confirmed in full**, but it is four names short: **Nippon Life** (own-brand products at roughly 70 regional institutions and nine securities firms), **Crédit Agricole Life** (USD fixed annuities with MVA at six banks), **Fukoku Shinrai Life** (the shinkin-bank line) and **Gibraltar Life** (independent agencies only, and under an FSA suspension order whose scope the company reads narrowly) all belong on the map. Within the ten, **ORIX** is the marginal name (one product, evidence partly snippet-level), **Manulife** is narrower than its brand suggests, **Meiji Yasuda** has no fixed annuity on sale and a shrinking bank channel, and **Nissay Wealth** is confirmed only from distributor pages because its own site blocked every read. Six insurers form a national tier that sits on megabank, regional and securities shelves alike: Dai-ichi Frontier, Mitsui Sumitomo Primary, Sumitomo Life, Nissay Wealth, MetLife and Meiji Yasuda. Five issuers sell single-premium fixed indexed annuities; **no tontine-style single-premium product was found** on any third-party shelf. Eleven insurers were screened with nothing in scope, four have an in-scope product but only in their own channel, and ten remain undetermined.

## How to read this map

The denominator is the Life Insurance Association of Japan member list dated 13 April 2026, which names 41 companies; an FSA survey annex dated 16 October 2026 names the same 41 under two older company names ([LIAJ](https://www.seiho.or.jp/member/list/), [FSA](https://www.fsa.go.jp/news/r8/hoken/20261016/20261016.html)). The FSA licence register itself was not fetched, so the 41 is a membership count, not a proven licence count ([LIAJ](https://www.seiho.or.jp/member/list/)).

Three layers of evidence sit behind every row, and each claim carries its tag. **[V]** means an independent verification pass read the insurer's own current list on 16 November 2026. **[v]** means the verifier read a distributor page. **[PR2]** means a second-round collector read the page directly but the verifier did not re-check it. **[F1]** means a first-round fetch whose text was summarised by a small model. **[S]** means a search snippet only. Where sources conflict, the verification wins, then the second-round page reads, then the first round. "On sale" means listed on the insurer's current list and not on its stopped or suspended list.

Scope is single premium (一時払) only: fixed annuities (定額個人年金), fixed indexed annuities (指数連動型年金), savings-focused whole life (一時払終身), and tontine-style annuities. Variable products, level-premium products and protection-focused whole life are out. Single-premium endowments (養老保険) are labelled borderline.

The carve-out is therefore the company's characterisation, not the regulator's wording. A first-round read of Gibraltar's rate page reported language saying new sales are suspended, which sits in tension with that reading [F1] ([Gibraltar rates](https://www.gib-life.co.jp/st/rate/)). The verification pass did not re-check the order, and no agency was confirmed to be taking applications.

## Conclusion

The working list was built around bank-channel specialists and captured them correctly; what it missed is that two parent insurers and two niche foreign-owned issuers also sit on third-party shelves. Nippon Life is the consequential addition, because its own-brand products run alongside those of its subsidiary Nissay Wealth across regional banks even as they leave megabanks. Product names turn out to be a poor guide to supply: MetLife sells two products under nine names, Dai-ichi Frontier reissues the same formal products as Mizuho, Resona, SMBC, Nomura and Shikoku editions, and Nissay Wealth's distributor names cannot be mapped at all. Any rate-tracking exercise should therefore key on the formal product name and the insurer's rate page, not on the shelf name.

Three developments could redraw the map within a year: the Gibraltar order runs to 31 January 2027, T&D Financial changes hands in October 2027, and yen products are returning to shelves as rates rise, which is the most likely route by which Tokio Marine & Nichido Anshin, Taiyo or Daido move from undetermined to in scope. Rate publication is the practical constraint for monitoring: Dai-ichi Frontier, MS Primary, Meiji Yasuda, T&D Financial, Nippon Life (GOLD3) and Crédit Agricole publish HTML tables that were read, PGF publishes PDFs, and Sumitomo and Nissay Wealth, two of the six national issuers, publish nothing that could be read.

*Update 2026-11-17:* that last point no longer holds. Sumitomo's `neth` rate pages and Nissay Wealth's rate pages (from a connection in Japan) are read and collected, as are MetLife's and Manulife's rates.
