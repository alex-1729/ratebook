# Japan savings-insurance product research

Competitive-intelligence dataset on Japanese single-premium savings products. It feeds a personal Electron desktop app (Mac and Windows) in `app/`.

## Scope

**Products (single premium only)**
- Fixed annuities (定額個人年金), 5-10 year terms, with or without MVA
- Fixed indexed annuities (指数連動型年金)
- Savings-focused whole life (一時払終身), including rate-reset (積立利率更改型) and target-setting (目標設定型) variants
- Single-premium tontine-style annuities

Out: variable annuities, level-premium products, protection-focused whole life.

**Currencies**: JPY, USD, AUD. Others are noted if seen, not pursued.

**Insurers**: Dai-ichi Frontier (pilot), Mitsui Sumitomo Primary, Sumitomo, T&D Financial, Orix, Nippon Wealth, Prudential Gibraltar Financial, Manulife Japan, MetLife Japan, Meiji Yasuda, Nippon Life, Crédit Agricole Life. All twelve have at least one product with collected rates. `data/insurers/` also holds insurers followed for news only. Nippon Wealth is partly tracked by policy form (`nwl-form-*`), because its rate pages are organised by form; the marketed names are mapped to forms from the insurer's own product pages.

**Products on file**: 39 in `data/products/`. 8 are tracked: shown in the app everywhere. 31 are candidates (`"candidate": true`): their rates are collected like the others, but the app hides them until they are enabled on its Candidates page (see `app/README.md`).

**Data captured**
- Rate time series, full published grid (currency, term, premium band, age band, distributor where it varies): 積立利率, 予定利率, FIA 連動率 / cap / index
- Product attributes: loads, MVA terms, net or target yields, on-sale and off-sale dates
- Distributors per product, with the bank's own product name where white-labelled
- Names in Japanese (canonical) with an English gloss

**History**: as far back as recoverable. On-sale products first, then discontinued ones. Gaps are recorded, never interpolated.

## Rules

- Public primary sources only: insurer and distributor sites, plus the Wayback Machine. Trade press for launch dates only.
- Every data point carries a source URL and retrieval date. Wayback-derived values are flagged.
- The raw snapshot (PDF or HTML) each number came from is saved under `sources/`.
- Structured sources are collected by re-runnable Python scripts in `scripts/`. Hand transcription is for one-off facts only.
- An agent that did not collect the data re-checks about 10% per insurer against the snapshots, plus all outliers.

## Layout

- `data/insurers/` - insurers (JSON)
- `data/products/` - one JSON per product: names, policy names, product type, plans, pages, documents, distributors, notes. Sale fields: `status`; `on_sale_date` (a day, or a month such as `"2017-08"` where only the month is sourced) with `on_sale_date_source`; `on_sale_not_later_than` with its source, a bound where no launch is sourced; `off_sale_date`; `off_sale_between`, two archive captures that bracket an undated withdrawal; `partial_withdrawals`, courses or cells closed to new business while the product stays on sale; and `sale_status_checked`, the date and snapshot of the last on-sale check
- `data/product_types.json` - the product types used to group products
- `data/rates/` - one long-format CSV per product, metric and plan
- `data/markets/` - government par yields (`govt_yield__<CCY>.csv`, from `scripts/govt_yields.py`) and each rate's spread over them (`rate_spreads.csv`, generated by `scripts/rate_spreads.py` and not in git)
- `data/news/` - news items (`items/*.jsonl`), stories (`stories/`), market-wide sources (`sources/`) and the last check of each source (`state.json`)
- `sources/` - raw snapshots by insurer: `pages/`, `rates/`, `wayback/`, and `docs/` (PDFs are kept on disk but not in git; `docs/manifest.csv` lists them with URL and checksum). News-release PDFs saved under `pages/` as launch-date evidence are likewise not in git; each product's `on_sale_date_source` gives the URL and path
- `scripts/` - collectors, one per insurer, plus `collect_all.py`; `news.py`, `news_enrich.py` and the site readers in `news_sites/` for news; `news_migrate_ids.py`, which moved the news records onto the rate-side insurer ids (one id per company)
- `scripts/check_offline_parse.py`, `scripts/recheck_candidates_a.py`, `scripts/recheck_candidates_b.py` - offline re-checks of the rate CSVs against the saved snapshots (see Collecting); notes in `research_notes/recheck-candidates-a.md` and `-b.md`
- `scripts/value/` - like-for-like value model (返戻率, surrender path, MVA and FX effects from published rates); see `reports/Like-for-like value.md`
- `app/` - Electron viewer for the rates and documents (see `app/README.md`)
- `reports/`, `research_notes/` - write-ups

## Collecting

```
python3 scripts/collect_all.py           # rates for every insurer
python3 scripts/collect_all.py --docs    # also refresh documents
```

`collect_all.py` sorts the insurers three ways: direct, restricted and saved.

- **Direct**: Dai-ichi Frontier, Sumitomo Life, T&D Financial, Nippon Life, Meiji Yasuda, ORIX Life, Crédit Agricole Life and MetLife.
- **Restricted**: Mitsui Sumitomo Primary (three products: MAREⅢ, Broadway WorldⅢ and あしたの、よろこび2, one rate page each) and Nippon Wealth answer only from some networks, for example a connection in Japan (a Japan VPN works). `collect_all.py` probes them first and reports them as blocked when they do not answer. When they do, it reads them live: it saves the current pages and ingests them. On its own, `msp.py` reads the MS Primary rate server live and `nwl.py --live` reads Nippon Wealth. Elsewhere, `scripts/snapshot_jp_rates.py` saves their pages on a machine that can reach them, and `msp.py --file` / `nwl.py --live-dir` ingest the saved copies.

**Saved**: PGF Life and Manulife are collected from saved copies only: no request is made to their sites. Their rate tables were read in a browser and written out as page copies under `sources/pgf/` and `sources/mnl/`; `pgf.py` and `mnl.py` only re-read those copies, so a run adds nothing until a newer copy is saved. These copies are transcriptions, not raw pages: treat both insurers' history as provisional. The current-period values were compared with the live pages on 2026-11-17 and matched.

`python3 scripts/fetch_docs.py` downloads the documents listed in any product file into `sources/<insurer>/docs/` and records them in that insurer's `manifest.csv`. The PDFs stay out of git.

`python3 scripts/restore_docs.py` puts those PDFs back on a fresh clone, or after losing the files. It downloads every file the manifests list and saves one only if its checksum matches; a document the insurer has since replaced is reported and not saved. The same goes for the banks' product-list PDFs saved as evidence of who sells a product, listed in `sources/<insurer>/distributors/manifest.csv`. It also re-fetches the news-release PDFs named in product files, which have no checksum and are saved as unverified. `--dry-run` lists what is missing without making a request. Files already on disk are never overwritten. A site that refuses the request is reported and left, so run it again later or from another network: on 2026-11-17 a full run restored 194 of 213 files, with MetLife answering 403 for its ten brochures and the Wayback Machine throttling the nine files that come from its captures.

Existing data points are never rewritten: a collector reports a conflict if a source now shows a different value.

Every product, candidates included, carries a `verification` block saying how its rates were re-checked against the saved snapshots and up to which retrieval date. For the eight tracked products an independent parser compared every row on 2026-11-16 and 17. The 31 candidates were checked by `scripts/recheck_candidates_a.py` and `scripts/recheck_candidates_b.py`: each has its own parsing, imports nothing from the collectors, and checks a seeded 10% sample per product plus every outlier (notes in `research_notes/recheck-candidates-a.md` and `-b.md`). `scripts/check_offline_parse.py` is a separate check that the collectors' own parsers, fed the saved snapshots, reproduce the CSVs.

29 of the 39 products have an on-sale date with its source: 28 to the day and one (Meiji Yasuda's 外貨建・エブリバディプラス, `2017-08`) to the month only. 4 more have only a "not later than" bound (`on_sale_not_later_than`). For the other 6 the date is open and the searches made are recorded in the product's notes. Sale status was re-checked on 2026-11-17: 32 products on sale, 6 on sale with partial withdrawals, 1 discontinued (evidence in `research_notes/gaps/`).

## News

The tracker follows news for all 41 members of the Life Insurance Association, at three depths set by `news_tier` in each `data/insurers/<id>.json`:

- **full** (15): the insurers with an in-scope product on a third-party shelf, plus Prudential Life. Every item on their own news lists is collected, and they are covered by the press sweep.
- **watch** (6): their lists are collected, but only product, suspension, regulatory and similar items are shown.
- **market** (20): no list of their own. They appear when the FSA, the association or the press names them.

Moving an insurer between tiers is a one-line change to `news_tier` (a full or watch insurer also needs `news_sources`).

```
python3 scripts/news.py collect              # read every list, add new items
python3 scripts/news.py collect --enrich     # and add English titles, topics and summaries
python3 scripts/news.py status               # when each list was last read
python3 scripts/news.py add FILE --to press  # add press items found by a search sweep
```

- `news.py collect` is plain Python with no model: it reads the list pages named in `news_sources` (and in `data/news/sources/` for the FSA, the association and parent groups), and stores the date, Japanese title and link of each new item. Items on file are never rewritten; a changed title is reported as a conflict.
- `news_enrich.py` runs Claude Code with your sign-in (no API key) to add an English title, a topic, an importance mark and links to tracked products and stories, and a short summary for high-importance items. A fetched release is read and discarded; only the summary is kept.
- Press coverage is not scraped. A weekly search sweep adds items with `news.py add`: headline, link, publisher, date and a summary in the tracker's own words, never the article's text. This widens the "trade press for launch dates only" rule above for news items only; rates and product facts still come from primary sources.
- A **story** (`data/news/stories/<id>.json`) is a named thread such as a regulatory case: an overview, key dates, open questions, and the items that carry its id in `story_ids`.
- Known gaps on 2026-11-17: Gibraltar Life, PGF Life, Manulife and Nippon Wealth Life refuse requests from this network (bot challenge or HTTP 403), and ORIX Life's lists are filled in by script from a keyed service, so none of the five has items from its own lists yet. Their sources are configured; `news.py collect --file KEY=PATH` ingests a list page saved from a browser. For Meiji Yasuda, Sumitomo Life and Mitsui Sumitomo Primary the site readers follow further list pages without saving them, so only the first page of each list is in `sources/`.
- An independent check on 2026-11-17 re-read every item of the three stories and the press sweep against its source (items carry `verified`), and compared a 10% sample of collected items per owner with the saved list pages.
- Some sites refuse automated requests from some networks. They are reported as blocked, shown as needing attention in the app, and nothing is worked around.

## Translating brochures

`scripts/repaint/` turns a Japanese brochure PDF into an English one that keeps each page's layout: the Japanese text is removed and English is set in the same places. It needs its own Python environment and a Claude Code sign-in (no API key).

```
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r scripts/repaint/requirements.txt -r scripts/repaint/requirements-checks.txt
.venv/bin/python scripts/repaint/run.py SRC.pdf --all --work translations/_work/NAME --out translations/NAME.en.pdf --translator claude
```

Per page: `extract.py` reads the page, a model writes the English as units following `translator_prompt.md`, `layout.py` sets it, and `checks.py` judges the rendered result. Simple lines are settled by rule, repeated text comes from a translation memory in `translations/_memory/`, and identical pages are reused, so a brochure takes about ten minutes the first time and a few minutes for another edition. `translations/` is not in git.

The output is a working translation for personal reading. Most pages carry some layout defects that the checks flag (tight labels, text touching a box edge), and pages printed in very small type come out cramped.
